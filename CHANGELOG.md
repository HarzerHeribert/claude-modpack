# Changelog

## 0.9.0 (2026-10-19)

smart-compact's compaction after a turn is instant: its summary is written in the background before it is needed.

- **smart-compact 0.3.0: the summary written ahead** (`prepare`, on whenever `afterTurnPayback` is). When a compaction after a turn is due, a fork of the session writes the summary in the background as the turn ends, from the session's own cache; the compaction then takes milliseconds: the summary, then every message since, as they are. Live, in an interactive session at about 70k tokens: 16 to 19 s writing in the background, 3 to 5 ms compacting, and the next turn answered from the summary, also when a prompt came in before the summary was ready. Against `/compact` from the same 224k session (six tasks, three runs each; `compact_bench.py` arm PREP, `results-prep.jsonl`): full marks in 16 of 18 runs against 14 of 18 (mean 0.981 against 0.917), the next task started on 36k tokens instead of 42k and cost 14% less, less on all six. It compacts through `/compact`, since a mod's own `$.session.compact` passes every compaction hook but that mod's, the one that answers with the summary: the transcript shows that `/compact` and the summary as one line, whole under ctrl+o. A summary the conversation no longer matches is dropped with a line saying why, and Claude Code summarizes as before.
- **smart-compact learns only from its own compactions.** 0.2.0 also learned from Claude Code's, which keep a long tail of messages as they are, so a compaction looked worth less than it is and could stop being chosen at all.
- **modpack 0.9.0**: the same eight mods.
- **Benchmarks**: the runs of the summary written ahead (`results-prep.jsonl`) and the bench-only mod that installs it (`prep-mod/`).

## 0.8.1 (2026-10-18)

route says what it is: an optional mod, the one that needs an outside classifier model, measured now on real prompts.

- **route 0.3.2: Jev hands off from a score of 0.95.** Measured on 877 real Claude Code prompts (the author's sessions and public trace sets; anything that looked like a credential or personal data was left out before sending), against labels Haiku gave by route's rubrics: at route's 0.7, 86% of what Jev handed off was feature or test work, 51% on the author's interactive prompts; at 0.95, 95% and 78%. Jev scores route's own feature and test prompts 1.0, so they still get the order. ScaleDown keeps 0.7 (92% and 70%, finding fewer). `minScore` now defaults to 0, the classifier's own; a number you set still wins.
- **What route is worth in real sessions**: in the author's, the turns Jev hands off were 13% of the spend, so at most 4 to 7% saved at the benchmark's savings on handed-off features and tests, nearer 3 to 6% with the hand-offs that were not feature or test work. The benchmark's 14% came from a task mix in which a feature and tests were the dearest jobs.
- **Onboarding says it**: the README, the guided installer's summary, `/modpack:setup`, SETUP.md, route's own setup and `/mods` call route optional, name the outside classifier and its key it needs, and say what it was measured to be worth.
- **Local classifiers, tried and not shipped**: a 64 KB classifier trained on prompts Haiku wrote, then taught by Jev on 2,817 prompts, and LAYA (an open 421M-parameter decision model with Jev's request shape) zero-shot. All handle clean prompts; on the author's interactive prompts none came close to Jev (precision 0.17 to 0.21; the Jev-taught one found 9 of Jev's 61 hand-offs). A prompt in the middle of a session ("ok, build it like that") is feature work only with its conversation.

## 0.8.0 (2026-10-18)

draft joins the pack: writing to a word limit in one draft, not in rounds of rewrites. smart-compact can compact after a turn when that pays back soon (off until it has run in real sessions). eta names a background job by what it runs.

- **draft 0.1.3** (new, on): asked for at most 700 words, Claude wrote a whole guide at 1,052 words, counted, and wrote it whole again three times (798, 757, 694); every rewrite costs the whole text in output and stays in the conversation. When a prompt sets a word limit ("at most 700 words", "under 250 words", "a 150-word summary", "höchstens 400 Wörter"), draft adds your setting beside it: give each part its share and aim for about 85% of the limit, keep every fact the request asks for and cut wording, and if those facts cannot all fit, keep them and say how far over it runs; a draft still well over is written once more to each section's target, not cut in rounds. After each Write or Edit of a Markdown or text file, one line: its words as `wc -w` counts them, and each section's target when more than a tenth over. Without a limit it does nothing. Against the pack, on six writing tasks (a runbook, a guide revised to 700 words, a 200-word brief, a 300-word FAQ, a 2,080-word document cut to 800, a data report without a limit): −11.4% [−15.4, −7.2] for 0.1.2 (48 runs) and −10.7% [−14.9, −6.3] for 0.1.3 (24 runs), scores 0.990 and 0.987 against 0.990, no run over its limit. On the way: cutting in place with Edit (0.1.0) cost more than one more write, since an Edit repeats the text it changes; aiming at three quarters of the limit (0.1.1) dropped facts from the FAQ. `/draft:setup` checks it.
- **smart-compact 0.2.0: compaction after a turn** (`afterTurnPayback`, off by default). A compaction costs about $0.34 on Opus at 224k (the summary and the rewrite of what is left) and saves about $0.036 on every later request; with `afterTurnPayback: 20`, smart-compact compacts 20 s after a turn when that pays back within 20 requests, priced from the model, the cache's lifetime and what earlier compactions cost. Never during a turn, over a draft in the prompt box or while agents run. Measured from a 224k session (72 runs): no quality lost, also after three compactions in a row and with Haiku writing the summary (as good, no cheaper: it has no cache of the session); on short tasks that ended there it cost more (+11% to +72%), the payback never coming; in the author's sessions 62% of prompts past 250k were followed by 20 or more requests. Off until it has run in real sessions. Also fixed: the guard against compacting over a draft in the prompt box was never wired; idle compaction now checks it too.
- **eta 0.2.3**: a background job without a description is named by the command that does the work, not by the `cd` and variables in front of it.
- **modpack 0.8.0**: installs draft; `/mods` lists eight.
- **route, measured on real prompts**: a classifier trained to run locally got route's 25 prompts right and failed on 918 real ones (precision 0.18 on the author's interactive prompts); the outside classifiers have not been tested on real prompts. The README says so; route stays off by default.
- **Benchmarks**: three more writing tasks with tight limits (`docs_repo.py` doc-4 to doc-6, a base rebuilt whenever the module that builds it changes), the draft runs (`results-draft2.jsonl`, `results-draft3.jsonl`), and the compaction benchmark (`compact_bench.py`, `results-compact.jsonl`): three prepared sessions of 224k, tasks that need files, the conversation or nothing, five arms.

## 0.7.0 (2026-10-18)

lsp-plus leaves the pack; the pack measured beyond click; background hand-offs that no longer wait above your prompt; idle sessions kept warm through the night.

- **lsp-plus retired.** Measured twice against the pack without it, 120 runs in all, the pack cost no more without it: −1.8% [−10.4, +8.1] on renames and traces in click, a TypeScript library and a React app (48 runs), −1.6% [−7.2, +4.1] on web, operating and prose work (72 runs), every run at full marks but seven spread over all arms. Meanwhile it kept a language server running per language in every session, from the first prompt to the last: hundreds of MB each, 3.5 GB for pyright in our own lab repository. Its grep answer came from the language server, and in untyped code a server finds about half the uses of a name (pyright 50% on 49 names from Django and sympy; the code graphs Graphify and Trail-Code 47-49%), where a word search finds all of them and the files' outlines sort them into definitions and uses at 98%; with seek in the pack, the rename it once shortened took 3.3 requests with it and 3.7 without. **The update removes it**: modpack 0.7.0 uninstalls `lsp-plus@claude-modpack` at its first start, once, and says so in one line; installed again on purpose, it stays. New installs no longer get it, and the installer no longer sets up language servers. Its last version, 0.4.2, stays in the v0.6.1 tag. [Lab notes 12](lab-notes/12-the-mod-that-left.md) has the story.
- **Beyond click.** Twelve new tasks in the shape of Claude Code's most common work (Anthropic's study of 400,000 sessions): five in a React and TypeScript app (a search through the API and the URL, a planted bug reported as a user would, a rename whose namesakes must stay, a Tailwind breakpoint, the login session explained), four operating a small service (an import that fails 2,266 rows into a file, three config faults one after another, a port across eight kinds of file, a 60,000-line access log), three in prose and data (a runbook from notes and a chat log, a guide cut to 700 words, a CSV with a trap). The pack as 0.7.0 ships it against plain Claude Code: −17.2% [−22.1, −11.7] (48 runs): web code −28.1%, operating software −8.0%, prose and data −4.8% [−9.9, +0.6]. Prose is where the pack does least. The tasks ship in [`benchmarks/`](benchmarks/delegate-bench/) with hidden references and a selftest each (`web_repo.py`, `ops_repo.py`, `docs_repo.py`).
- **delegate 0.4.4: a background report never waits pinned above your input.** A hand-off that ended while Claude was working used to send its report as a prompt, which Claude Code queues above the input line until the turn ends, and then swapped the host's own notice for "already reached Claude". Now the report rides on the turn's next tool result or on that notice, and goes out as a prompt only when the session is idle. Its diff holds only the worker's own files (what you or another worker changed meanwhile is named in one line), with 40 lines of it and the path to the rest. Before its worker starts, while delegate looks up where the task reaches, a hand-off shows one line (its kind and the task's first line) instead of the call's whole input.
- **smart-compact 0.1.3: an idle session stays warm for up to 12 hours** (`maxIdleHours`, was 6). An open session is one its person means to come back to. In the author's sessions the gaps over 6 hours held most of what the keep-alive rule left to gain: 13% less per turn end at 12 hours (in sample, 24 such gaps). For a 5-minute cache the rule's own budget still ends keep-alives within the hour.
- **modpack 0.7.0**: installs and updates seven mods; removes a retired one once; `/mods` lists the seven.
- **README**: seven mods; the numbers beyond click; why lsp-plus left; language servers optional. [SETUP.md](SETUP.md): language servers optional, and how to keep pyright to what git tracks.

## 0.6.0 (2026-10-16)

Hand-offs that cost Claude two steps, checked by the tool itself; route handing feature and test work over before Claude reads the code; and seek, a new mod that has Claude look around in few, lean steps.

- **seek 0.3.3** (new, on): every request re-reads the whole conversation, and whatever Claude reads stays in it, re-read by every later request until a compaction (a median of 143 later requests in the author's interactive sessions). So beside your prompt seek adds your setting to look around in few, lean steps: the searches and reads Claude can already name in one command, definitions or line ranges instead of whole files, nothing read again that the session already holds. From the first prompt of a session, and again after a compaction or another 150k tokens. Against the pack in the same batch: −11.9% [−17.2, −5.9] fresh on click (20 runs each way), −16.4% [−21.8, −10.5] on TypeScript (24 each, 72 of 72 full marks), −10.2% [−17.6, −2.2] in a long session that had to find the code, −15.9% [−22.5, −8.6] in one that had read it all (24 each). The map of where a search's hits sit (`map`) was measured too and stays off: nothing certain saved. Nothing leaves your machine. `/seek:setup` checks it.
- **delegate 0.4.3: a verified hand-off.** The worker ends its report with a VERIFY block (the command that runs only its new tests, and what it left open). delegate runs that command beside the check, counts both outputs (no test selected is no pass), and its result starts with one line: Verified, with what passed, or Not verified, with each reason. On Verified, Claude reports without running anything again: one step after a hand-off instead of two or three. A read hand-off ends with a PLACES block instead (`path:line symbol` per place); delegate checks each place in the code and puts Verified or Not verified first. Read hand-offs measured +5% against Claude doing the lookup itself, so this is a safety net for the answer, not a saving: lookups still belong with Claude. The worker now starts in the session's project directory and is told the project's root (before, a worker could find another checkout of the same project on the disk and answer from it), and the diff runs git from the repository's top, so new files outside the session's folder are in it. A worker's report arriving a second time inside a running turn now rides along as one line, without the yellow "Prompt dropped by a hook" row.
- **route 0.3.1**: TypeSafe's Jev as a second classifier (`classifier: auto` takes it when its key is set): as accurate as ScaleDown on 50 test prompts and 4 to 5 times faster (0.25 s), and certain on a long feature spec ScaleDown scored under the threshold. The order now hands the work over before Claude reads the code (`direct`, on): followed 24 of 24 times, where Claude allowed to look around first kept the work 5 times in 16. Only the first 8,000 characters of a prompt are sent. On TypeScript, against the pack in the same batch, all correct: a feature 51% cheaper and 42% faster, Claude's own requests 11 → 2; writing tests 36% cheaper.
- **cells 0.4.3, seek 0.3.3: subagents get the settings too.** A subagent Claude starts has a system prompt of its own and never sees your prompts, so cells' chaining guidance and seek's setting never reached one. Now each adds one line to a subagent's task, in your name, under "The person's settings for this work:" (`subagents`, on; not forks, teammates or delegate's workers). With every task handed to one general-purpose subagent, 5 tasks × 4 runs per arm, Opus: −17.6% [−21.0, −14.0] per run, edit tasks −21.8%, explanations −7.8%; the subagent's requests 10.0 → 6.9, its Bash calls chained 88% → 99%. The lines reached 20 of 20 subagents with the option on, none with it off.
- **modpack 0.6.0**: installs seek; an update from an earlier release installs it as a mod new in the release.
- **Setup, step by step**: `/modpack:setup` checks the machine, asks every choice in one message, sets the mods up in order (route last, and only with your consent), and ends with a table of what is on and why; every mod has its own `/<mod>:setup` that ends with a check proving it works in your project (statusline 0.2.1, smart-compact 0.1.2, eta 0.2.2, lsp-plus 0.4.1, cells 0.4.3 carry theirs, seek its own); [SETUP.md](SETUP.md) is the same by hand.
- **Benchmarks: what a run leaves behind.** A bench run ends after its task, so what it added to the context was never read again; in a real session every later request re-reads it. [`carry.py`](benchmarks/delegate-bench/carry.py) prices that beside each run's cost (the tokens a run added × 140 later requests × the cache-read price). Counted this way, seek's earlier versions, which attached the lines around a search's hits, cost more than they saved (+6.3% fresh) and were retired; seek 0.3.1's setting saves more (−18% fresh, −23% on TypeScript).

The lab notes moved to [notes.html](https://harzerheribert.github.io/claude-modpack/notes.html), each page at its old address.

## 0.4.0 (2026-10-14)

A guard against other plugins' hooks that hang, `/mods` from the keyboard, and rows above the prompt that always end.

- **modpack 0.4.0**: a hook guard. A permission or notification hook of another plugin that runs over 10 seconds no longer freezes Claude Code: the pack stops waiting for it, Claude Code goes on (with a permission question, it asks you itself), and you are told once which plugin it was. Found with Warp's plugin, whose permission hook hangs outside the Warp terminal. Only these two kinds of hook, whose skipping is safe; a hook that guards tool calls is never touched. `hookGuard: off` turns it off, for a hook that waits for you on purpose. Checked live, including the freeze that started it: a delegate worker that needs your permission, behind a hook that hangs on purpose. Without the guard no question appeared for more than a minute; with it the question came after 10 s, in default and in auto mode, and the worker finished once approved.
- **modpack 0.4.0**: `/mods <mod> on`, `/mods <mod> off`, or `/mods <mod>` to switch: the pane's buttons answer only a mouse click.
- **eta 0.2.1**: a background command run by a subagent (a delegate worker) no longer gets a row that can never end: its end is reported to the subagent, so eta never heard it. A row that measures progress and gets no output for 20 minutes ends as "no update for 20 min".
- **delegate 0.2.1**: the line Claude Code shows when delegate drops the host's second copy of a worker's report now says what happened.
- **README**: real recordings of each feature, and the measured numbers animated.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-13)

The first public release: the `modpack` plugin and the six mods it installs.

- **statusline**: one line under the prompt with the model and its effort, how full the context is, the plan's 5-hour and 7-day use with their resets, and what other mods put on it through `$.statusline.set`.
- **smart-compact**: keeps an idle session's prompt cache warm, or compacts it just before it expires, whichever is cheaper; says on the line whether the next prompt is ready or cold.
- **delegate**: a `hand_off` tool that gives a self-contained task to a cheaper worker (Haiku or Sonnet), with the diff, a check it runs itself and the cost as evidence.
- **lsp-plus**: references and definitions that name the docs using a symbol, a warning when an edit leaves the docs behind, `/refs` for stale names and dead links, Markdown and SQL language servers, and a note when a grep looks for a code symbol.
- **cells**: a `run_cell` tool for several edits and their check in one step through the real Edit tool, the language server's references, definitions and renames, and a short note when a model request did nothing a previous one couldn't have.
- **eta**: long-running and background jobs with a percentage and an estimated finish time above the prompt; what has finished leaves, a failure stays until you write again.

How each was measured is in [`benchmarks/`](benchmarks/).
