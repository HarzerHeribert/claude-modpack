Why every step costs

Three things we learned while building the pack, in the order we learned them. Each one changed what we built.

Every step re-reads everything

A Claude Code session is a loop. Claude reads the conversation so far and takes one step: it opens a file, runs a search or makes an edit. The result joins the conversation, and then the whole conversation goes back to the model for the next step.

Caching makes that re-read cheap per word, but not free. In a session of 220,000 tokens, each step costs about four cents$0.044$0.044
Worked out, not measured
Opus 5.5, 15 Oct 2026 before any thinking happens. So the unit of cost is the step. Making each answer a little shorter saves a little; taking out a step saves a whole re-read of everything.

Our first measurement came out exactly along that line. The words per step barely moved, and the steps fell: 5.2 steps per job with the pack, against 7.2 without5.2 against 7.25.2 against 7.2

What a step leaves behind

Then we found the second half of the bill. Whatever a step reads stays in the conversation, and every later step reads it again, until the session is compacted.

In the author’s own sessions, a token added to the conversation was typically read again by 143143143 later steps. Writing it costs once; over its life, re-reading it costs more than four times as much$37$37

That sank the first versions of seek: they saved steps by adding text, and the text outlived the steps it saved. The version that ships asks Claude to read less, and leaves less behind.

So the pack’s mods do not hand Claude advice. They speak in your name beside your prompt, like delegate, route and seek, or they do the work themselves, like cells.
