mods / seek

seek

Look around in few, lean steps.

costv0.3.3−11.9%fresh, against the pack

The problem

Before Claude changes anything, it looks around: a search, a read, another search. Each look is a step that re-reads the conversation, and what it reads stays in the conversation, read again by every later step until a compaction removes it.

What it changes

Beside the first prompt of a session, and again after a compaction or another 150k tokens, seek passes on your setting to look around in few, lean steps: the searches Claude can already name in one go, the lines that matter rather than whole files, nothing read twice. It runs on your machine and sends nothing anywhere.

See it work

The order, and when it comes

seek’s text beside your prompt.

When it comes

The order’s text and its clock as seek writes them: with the first prompt (20 characters or more, not a slash command), again once the context has grown by another 150k tokens, and after a compaction. The session is an example.

What a step leaves behind

A step’s output stays in the context and is read again.

Worked out from Opus 5.5’s list prices: $0.20 per million tokens read from the cache, $8 to write them (one-hour lifetime). List prices. 143 is the median of later requests in the author’s interactive sessions (22 in headless ones): lab notes 11.

Words used here: carry, order and advice, context, compaction.

What did not make it

  1. Windows after a search

    retiredCost more than it saved+2.1%

    The idea. The first seek attached the lines around a search's hits to the search's result, so Claude would not need a request of its own to read them. Why it looked good: In real sessions the most common predictable step after a search was opening a file it had just listed.

    What stopped it. seek 0.1 fired in 1 of 36 runs: Opus's searches sit behind a cd, in chains and in pipes. seek 0.2 fired, saved 7.9% of the requests and cost +2.1% fresh; priced with what the windows left in the context, +6.3% [+1.4, +11.2].

    Now. Retired. Replaced by an order at the prompt. Count what stays: a step saved by adding text is paid for again by every later request.

  2. An order in the middle of a search

    droppedClaude ignored it−3.4%

    The idea. pathfinder waited for the third consecutive search in a large context and then told Claude to hand the rest of the search to a cheaper read worker. Why it looked good: Long chains of looking around, mostly in contexts over 400k tokens, held 10 to 16% of the cost.

    What stopped it. The order came in 6 of 12 runs; Claude handed off once. Cost −3.4% [−11.3, +5.1], not shown.

    Now. In the graveyard. The same kind of order beside the person's prompt is followed every time; inside Claude's own search it is not.

    off by defaultNo saving we could show−4.5%
